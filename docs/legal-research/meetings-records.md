## MTG-1 — Who receives annual-meeting notice?

| Entity | Confirmed statutory rule and citation | Variation and application |
|---|---|---|
| BC society | BC-SA **77(1)** requires written notice to **every member**; it is not limited to voting members. An appointed auditor has a separate notice and attendance right under **118(1)**. | Non-voting membership is not a reason to omit notice. Section 77 does not separately require every non-member director to be notified, although a director may be a member and bylaws can add notice recipients. For a requisitionist-called meeting, **75(7)(b)** expressly also includes each director. Confirm who remains a member under the bylaws before excluding a suspended or delinquent person. |
| BC company | BC-BCA **169(1)(a)–(b)**: each shareholder **entitled to attend** and each director. Auditor's separate right: **219(1)**. | Entitlement to attend is wider than an unexamined “voting shares” filter. Articles, class rights and the business of this particular meeting matter. Including all registered holders for review is safer than silently dropping non-voting classes. Auditor notice is not s 213; that section concerns an audit opinion. |
| Federal CBCA | [**135(1)**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-135.html): each shareholder entitled to vote, every director and the auditor. | Apply the notice record date and any statutory class-voting entitlement. A non-voting shareholder can become entitled to vote on specified fundamental changes. Annual financial-statement distribution under **159(1)** has a different, broader audience; do not reuse the meeting-notice filter. |
| Ontario OBCA | **96(1)(a)–(c)**: shareholders entitled to vote, each director and the auditor. **96(2)** addresses the notice record date and protects voting rights despite nonreceipt. | For an offering corporation, ordinary notice minimum is 21 days; for other corporations, 10 days; maximum is 50 days. Separate class votes and articles/agreements need review. Annual statements have their own rules in 154(3)–(4). |

**Confidence:** confirmed general law; organizational and meeting-specific entitlement is conditional on the legal register and governing documents. A workspace login, contact email or “shareholder” role label is not proof of share ownership. A statutory auditor is not every accountant, bookkeeper or compilation-service provider.

**Implementation:** society notice helpers now include active non-voting members. Corporate helpers add recorded active directors and appointed auditors, preserve stable recipient identifiers and retain unresolved class/register warnings. BC company audiences conservatively include non-voting registered holders for attendance-rights review. They do not certify that a current share register equals the statutory historical notice list.

## MTG-2 — Delivery methods, electronic notice and proof

| Entity | Statutory methods and conditions | Required product distinction |
|---|---|---|
| BC society | BC-SA **29–30**, **77–79** govern giving notices, required content and waiver. Section **77(2)** permits the alternative email-plus-publication method only for a society with **more than 100 members**, where bylaws permit it: email each member whose email is recorded, plus the specified newspaper publication or website posting. Website notice must be accessible to all members and remain posted for at least 21 days before, through the meeting. **78(2)** requires electronic participation/voting instructions where relevant. | The old 250-member threshold is obsolete. Posting alone is not a universal substitute for individual notice. The ordinary minimum is 14 days unless bylaws choose a period of at least 7; maximum 60 days. Waiver under 79 is a distinct event, not assumed from a sent email. |
| BC company | BC-BCA **7–9**, **169–170**; BC-BCR **4–5** allow email/fax to the address supplied for that record or class of records. BC-BCR **3(1)**: public company 21 days or longer articles period; private company the articles period if at least 10 days, otherwise 21. Maximum under 169 is **two calendar months**, not 60 or 62 days. | Keep supplied-address/consent evidence, governing-articles version and any waiver. Under BC-IA **25.3(1)(b), (3)**, calculate a period before a meeting backwards from the meeting date; e.g. August 31 → June 30. Forward addition produces wrong month-end results. Applicable clear-day and holiday provisions require their own treatment. |
| Federal CBCA | **135**, **253** and electronic-document Part XX.1. [**252.3(2)–(3)**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-252.3.html) require prescribed consent and a designated receiving information system, with revocation recognized; **252.4** and regulations supply further electronic requirements. | Merely possessing an email address is insufficient evidence of consent to electronic statutory delivery. Notice-and-access under securities rules is a conditional distribution regime; it is not blanket permission to post any private corporation's notice on a website. Retain the relevant consent, notice-and-access conditions or exemption if that route is used. |
| Ontario OBCA | **96**, service/electronic-document provisions of the Act, applicable regulations and Ontario's [Electronic Commerce Act, 2000](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/00e17). **96(1.1)–(1.2)** prescribe electronic-meeting participation and voting instructions and address fully electronic meetings. | Check the lawful delivery route and recipient's electronic-delivery arrangements; do not import BC's large-society website alternative. Current securities notice-and-access requirements must be separately assessed for offering corporations. |

**Evidence model (implementation recommendation, not a legislated universal form):** retain the final notice/attachments and special-resolution text; notice-list snapshot and record-date authority; recipient's register ID and capacity; actual dispatch date/time, method and address; address/consent provenance; provider message ID and status; returned mail/bounces and corrective delivery; separately recorded waivers; publication copies, dates and accessibility evidence when that lawful alternative is used. An opening pixel does not replace the statutory delivery test. A queued or skipped delivery is not sent. Duplicate dispatches to one person cannot cover omitted people. A manually reported total is useful audit information but cannot prove recipient-level coverage.

**Confidence:** confirmed statutory differences; applicability of a specific electronic route, address, waiver or public-market notice-and-access regime requires its facts and documents. Delivery success alone is not a legal-compliance certification.

### Nominal calendar boundaries and holiday review

The statutory interval and its adjusted calendar boundary are separate questions. A raw days/months comparison cannot certify notice timing or automatically classify a filing as legally late.

| Governing calendar authority | Confirmed rule and practical limit |
|---|---|
| BC-IA **2(1), 25(3)–(4), 25.2–25.5, 29** | For calculated periods, a holiday boundary moves forward when the reference day precedes the period and backward when the reference day follows it. Section25.5 moves a specified holiday deadline forward. Office closures have separate, direction-sensitive rules. Thus a boundary before a meeting may need an **earlier** date. Saturday is not automatically a holiday. Apply the governing enactment and any contrary intention. |
| FED-IA **3(1), 26–28, 35(1)** | A holiday expiry generally carries to the next non-holiday; day-counting and calendar-month rules remain distinct. The holiday definition can include provincial/local holidays. Review the precise notice provision and location; generic weekend movement does not establish sufficient notice. |
| ON-LA **47, 88–89**; OBCA **1(8)** | Holiday expiries generally carry to the next non-holiday; a required filing place closed during regular hours has a separate next-opening rule. OBCA's day-period rule expressly addresses midnight and holiday expiry. Use the applicable statutory holiday, proclamation and office/channel information, not a universal Saturday-to-Monday rule. |

For example, the BC society no-AGM report's nominal **January 31, 2027** deadline falls on Sunday. Under BC-IA25.5(1), subject to any applicable contrary provision, February1 remains timely. An unadjusted comparison would wrongly label a filing that day late. This example illustrates why nominal reporting dates require review; it does not supply an all-jurisdiction holiday calendar.

**Implementation:** `legalMeetingNotice.ts` keeps the original calendar arithmetic and explicitly labels its results nominal. `assessNoticeWindow` returns calendar review required and never a legally verified result; even a date inside the nominal range receives an informational posture. Scheduling forms expose the holiday/office caveat, and the sidebar records dispatch without asserting that notice was timely. Primary registry entries are `BC-IA-TIME`, `FED-IA-TIME`, `ON-LEGISLATION-TIME` and `OBCA-TIME`. The focused notice-calendar regression retains Sunday and month-end boundaries without guessed adjustments and rejects a green legal conclusion from raw arithmetic. **Confidence:** confirmed general interpretation rules; the actual adjusted boundary remains conditional on the applicable provision, calendar and delivery facts.

## MTG-3 — Electronic and hybrid meetings

| Entity | Confirmed rule | Governing-document exception |
|---|---|---|
| BC society | BC-SA **83(1)–(3)** permits electronic participation unless bylaws provide otherwise, with communication sufficient for participation. Where the society offers electronic participation, **83(2.1)** requires it to facilitate participation. | A physical-only meeting does not automatically oblige the society to supply an electronic channel. **76** separately addresses location; fully electronic meetings have the statutory location exception. Notice must contain required instructions under 78. |
| BC company | BC-BCA **174** permits electronic participation unless articles provide otherwise and requires sufficient communication; offered electronic meetings must be facilitated. | Check articles and participation mechanics, including the voting channel. Do not presume every physical-only meeting must become hybrid. |
| Federal CBCA | [**132(4)**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-132.html) permits participation through adequate electronic facilities unless bylaws say otherwise. **132(5)** requires bylaw permission for a meeting held **entirely** electronically. | Hybrid participation and fully virtual convening are distinct legal questions. A single “electronic meetings permitted by default” flag is insufficient for CBCA. |
| Ontario OBCA | **94(2)–(5)**, as amended effective October 1, 2023, permits hybrid or fully electronic meetings unless articles/bylaws expressly limit the means; participants must be able to participate reasonably. | Do not reuse the CBCA requirement for affirmative full-virtual bylaw permission. Apply notice instructions under 96 and location rules under 93. |

**Confidence:** confirmed. Record the actual communication/voting arrangement and relevant document provision; a video-link field does not establish compliance.

## MTG-4 — Proxies

| Entity | Default, form and duration | Limits and variation |
|---|---|---|
| BC society | BC-SA **85**: proxies are unavailable **unless bylaws permit them**. Appointment is in writing, for the specified meeting/adjournment unless bylaws allow otherwise, and revocable. The statutory default proxy holder is a member; the section permits specified bylaw variation. | This prohibition is statutory default, not merely the model bylaws. Do not derive a universal cap on how many grantors one proxy holder may represent. Check bylaws for proxy-holder qualification, duration and limits. |
| BC company | BC-BCA **173(1)** supports personal or proxy voting subject to articles; additional provisions govern proxies and their use. | Articles and, for relevant public/pre-existing reporting companies, statutory/securities proxy provisions control the instrument and procedure. Do not apply the society's default prohibition. A public-company proxy solicitation package and a private-company appointment are different deliverables. |
| Federal CBCA | [**148(1)–(5)**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-148.html): a voting shareholder may appoint a proxy holder and alternates, who need not be shareholders; appointment is signed by the shareholder or authorized personal representative. It is meeting-specific, with adjournments. Revocation and deposit deadlines are specified. The directors' deposit cutoff cannot exceed 48 hours, excluding Saturdays and holidays, before the meeting/adjournment. | **149–154** and the CBCR govern solicitation, forms and circulars, including the non-distributing corporation with 50 or fewer shareholders exception. There is no general one-proxy-per-holder rule. The form must accommodate the actual voting instructions and applicable mandatory contents. |
| Ontario OBCA | **110–114** establish appointment, execution, revocation, deposit and proxy-holder duties; the proxy holder need not be a shareholder. | Observe the statutory deposit-limit calculation and the applicable solicitation/form rules. No universal headcount cap should be invented. Review the bylaws, instrument, meeting and adjournment. |

**Product record:** distinguish grantor's legal holder ID, human proxy holder, authorizing signature/representative, meeting and class scope, execution, instructions, deposit deadline, revocation and replacement. A corporate shareholder's authorized representative is not necessarily the same legal capacity as a discretionary proxy holder. Joint holders must not produce duplicate voting units. **Confidence:** confirmed baseline; form contents vary by public/private regime and governing documents.

## MTG-5 — Share classes, record dates, voter counts and ties

**BC society:** BC-SA **84** gives each voting member one vote and allows specified membership-class/bylaw arrangements, including non-voting classes, good-standing restrictions, attribute-based director selection and permitted indirect/electronic methods. Voting rights must follow those valid arrangements. A count of members with a boolean flag is an operational projection requiring verification against the actual bylaws and legal register.

**BC company:** BC-BCA **171** distinguishes record dates for notice and voting and supplies defaults where directors do not fix one; the default includes the prescribed 5 p.m. point on the day before notice, or the meeting's commencement where no notice is sent. **173** supplies the ordinary one-vote-per-share starting point, subject to articles and class rights. **173(9)** supplies special voting treatment for shareholders entitled to vote on a matter despite ordinary class restrictions. The list and the business being voted on must drive eligibility.

**CBCA:** [**134**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-134.html), [**138**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-138.html), [**140**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-140.html) and CBCR record-date provisions govern the relevant lists, dates and rights. Default voting power is one per share unless articles otherwise provide. Corporate shareholders can act through the representative authorized under 140(2)–(3); joint-holder voting is addressed in 140(4). Do not delete organizations or minors from the ownership denominator because they cannot personally authenticate or because their legal capacity requires a representative.

**OBCA:** **95**, **100**, **102**. A fixed notice record date under 95(2) is 30–60 days before the meeting; the default is linked to the close of business before notice, or the meeting where no notice is given. Section **100(2)–(3)** includes the transferee procedure: after the relevant list/record date, a transferee may establish ownership and demand inclusion no later than ten days before the meeting, or the shorter bylaw period. That Ontario rule must not be copied into the current CBCA list rule. Default voting is one per share, subject to articles and statutory class rights.

**Ties:** a tie does not satisfy an ordinary-resolution requirement for a majority of votes cast. None of these general rules gives every chair an automatic extra vote. Any claimed casting vote, runoff, cumulative-election elimination or tie-selection procedure must be valid under the Act and governing documents. Reporting equal totals is different from declaring a director elected. Quorum, resolution threshold and election outcome are different calculations.

**Implementation:** `shareholderElectorate.ts` computes weights only from identified ledger positions and explicit or unambiguous class rights, aggregates by legal holder, retains class breakdowns and reports unresolved ownership/rights. `electionElectorate.ts` records the reviewed date, documentary bases and same-tenant active account authorized for each holder. Historical reconstruction uses posted effective-dated transactions and reconciles them to the current register. Opening freezes weights and representative assignments. The ballot receives its weight from the server; clients cannot choose a weight. Accounts can represent multiple separately identified legal holders. Email matching never establishes authority. Current or reconstructed positions still require a reviewer to verify statutory cutoff time, special class rights and any Ontario transferee demands; the ledger is not a registry certification.

**Confidence:** confirmed general statutory model; precise eligibility is conditional on the meeting's record-date authority, instruments and ownership evidence.

## MTG-6 — Cumulative elections and staggered terms

| Entity | Election model and term rule | Implementation consequence |
|---|---|---|
| BC society | BC-SA **42**, **84** and the bylaws determine election/appointment and terms. There is no general statutory cumulative-share-voting default. | Use member/delegate rights actually adopted. Do not import the corporate vote multiplier or a universal corporate term cap. |
| BC company | BC-BCA director provisions and articles, including **122–124**, determine how directors are selected and cease office. No general CBCA-style cumulative default should be inferred. | Read the articles, term and class election rights. The app must not state that all BC company directors automatically have CBCA three-year terms. |
| Federal CBCA | **106(3)–(5)** permits terms up to the close of the third annual meeting and staggered terms. Prescribed distributing corporations have additional annual/individual-election rules. [**107**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-107.html) makes cumulative voting conditional on the articles; each voter allocates base votes × number of directors to be elected. The cumulative regime also changes removal and term rules, including election through the next annual meeting. | Cumulative voting is not the default. A two-seat cumulative poll permits allocation across candidates within the total budget; it is not just two unweighted checkboxes. Do not combine ordinary staggered terms with the cumulative rule without checking 107. |
| Ontario OBCA | **119(4)–(6)** permits terms up to the third annual meeting and staggering. **120** provides cumulative voting when articles require it, with associated term/removal consequences. | Same need for a reviewed voting method and seat multiplier; do not assume an ordinary poll is cumulative. |

The election workflow supports weighted ordinary selections and reviewed cumulative allocations, enforces the per-holder budget, and reports totals. Appointment, qualification, mandatory individual ballots, valid term and tie determination require the applicable legal review. **Confidence:** confirmed framework; precise election mechanics are document-specific.

## MTG-7 — Member and shareholder proposals

| Entity | Eligibility and deadline | Content and exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| BC society | BC-SA **81**: normally signatures of at least **5% of voting members**, with bylaws permitted to lower the percentage, but **at least two voting members**. A compliant proposal received at least seven days before notice is sent must be handled through the statutory notice process. | Include proposal/supporting statement within the 500-word statutory limit, names/signatures and special-resolution text where required. The submitting member must be personally present as required by 81; a proxy does not supply that condition. Substantially the same matter considered in the prior two calendar years can be excluded under the section. |
| BC company | BC-BCA **187–191** statutory proposal machinery applies to **public companies**. Qualified holders/beneficial owners satisfy the two-year continuous holding rule. Under **188(1)**, submitter and qualified supporters aggregate at least 1% of issued voting shares or shares with fair market value **exceeding $2,000** (BC-BCR **17**). Delivery deadline is at least three months before the anniversary of the preceding annual reference date. | Proposal/supporting statement limit is 1,000 words. Required declarations, holdings and contact details matter. Sections 189 and 191 govern permitted refusal and notice of refusal; BC-BCR18 supplies prior-support rules. A private company's agreed agenda procedure is not automatically this public-company statutory right. |
| Federal CBCA | [**137**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-137.html); CBCR **46–53**. A qualified registered/beneficial holder, or aggregate supporters, must meet **1% of voting shares or $2,000 fair market value**, with six months' holding. [CBCR **49**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-512/section-49.html) now gives a **60-day submission period starting 150 days before the anniversary of the previous annual meeting**. | The combined proposal/supporting statement is 500 words under Reg48. Director-nomination proposals have the separate **5%** requirement in 137(4). Rejection grounds and prompt reasons are specified. The old deadline calculated from the anniversary of sending the previous notice is obsolete. |
| Ontario OBCA | **99** gives a voting registered or beneficial holder proposal rights; director-nomination proposal support is **5%** under 99(4). **99(5)(a)** uses a 60-day lead for offering-corporation annual anniversaries/special meetings. **99(5.1)** allows non-offering articles/bylaws/USA to specify a lead within **21–60 days**; the default is 60. | Under **99(5.2)**, a timely proposal received after notice was sent requires supplemental circulation at least ten days before the meeting. Content, supporting statement, refusal grounds and notification are governed by 99 and the regulation. Do not reuse BC society's 5%-and-two-signatures rule as Ontario's general eligibility threshold. |

**Confidence:** confirmed distinctions, including modern CBCA window. Implement separate eligibility, ownership-duration, proposal-content and deadline checks; do not reduce the entire process to `signatureCount >= 5%`.

## MTG-8 — Requisitioned meetings

| Entity | Threshold | Directors' deadline and requisitionists' route |
|---|---|---|
| BC society | BC-SA **75**: **10% of voting members**, or lower bylaw percentage. Business is limited to the statutory 500-word request; comply with signatures and delivery to the registered office and directors. | Directors must call promptly a meeting held within **60 days** of receipt. If not called within **21 days**, the requisite majority of requisitionists may call it within **60 days after that 21-day period expires**. Section 75 addresses notice and reimbursement, subject to the members' ordinary resolution. |
| BC company | BC-BCA **167**: holders of at least **5% of issued shares carrying the right to vote at the meeting**. Requisition identifies business, signatories and addresses; statutory maximum is 1,000 words. | Directors must call a meeting held within **four months**. If they fail to send notice within **21 days**, qualifying requisitionists holding **more than 2.5%** may call the meeting, within the statutory four-month period. Refusal grounds and cost reimbursement are specific, including repeated proposals and BC-BCR16 prior-support rules. |
| Federal CBCA | [**143(1)–(6)**](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-143.html): **5% of issued voting shares for that meeting**. Send the signed business request to the registered office and each director. | Directors normally call within **21 days**; otherwise any signing shareholder can call, subject to the statutory exceptions. Do not import BC's four-month deadline into CBCA. The corporation normally reimburses reasonable expenses unless shareholders resolve otherwise. |
| Ontario OBCA | **105**: **5% of issued shares carrying the relevant voting right**; send the business request to the registered office. | Directors have **21 days** to call, subject to specified exceptions, after which a signing shareholder can call. Section105(6)'s reimbursement exception includes lack of good faith and general shareholder interest. Do not substitute the society's 10% member threshold. |

“Five percent of shares” is not necessarily five percent of people or five percent of weighted voting power. Store the statutory denominator and the shares' entitlement for the meeting. **Confidence:** confirmed, with validity of the request and refusal grounds requiring review.

Statements remain required. Section160 concerns **interim** financial statements and is not the annual distribution provision. |

**Confidence:** confirmed general requirements. A lawyer/accountant's review of whether the specific organization qualifies for an exemption, and whether its evidence covers the correct year, remains a fact-dependent task. Store statement period, approval, signature, delivery, appointment/waiver, its electorate and expiry separately.

## MTG-10 — Minutes and signatures

| Entity | Statutory record obligation | Signing and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| BC society | BC-SA **20(1)–(2)** requires general-meeting and directors/committee records, including resolutions. Relevant disclosures/dissents and approvals must be recorded under their governing sections; financial approval is separately governed by 38. | The Act does not impose a universal chair-plus-secretary signature on every minute. Applicable bylaws, an adopted approval procedure and evidential needs may require or justify signatures. Preserve the approved version and approval event. |
| BC company | BC-BCA **42**, **179(1)**: retain minutes of shareholder proceedings and the prescribed board/committee records. Written consent/resolution records must also be retained. | **179(2)–(3)** gives minutes signed by the chair of that or the next meeting evidentiary effect. | Federal CBCA | **20(1)–(2)** requires shareholder, director and committee minutes/resolutions. **117(2)** and **142(2)** require written resolutions to be retained with the corresponding minutes. **120** and **123** affect conflict and dissent evidence. | No universal two-officer signature requirement. Meeting, written resolution, certified extract and attendance records are separate evidence types; accurately identify which is being signed. |
| Ontario OBCA | **140(1)(b), (2)** requires shareholder and director/committee minutes and resolutions; **104** and **129** govern written substitutes, and **132**, **135** relevant conflict/dissent records. | **266** supplies a certificate/certified-extract evidence mechanism, not a universal rule requiring two signatures on original minutes. Check governing documents and approval procedure. |

Recommended content: entity and meeting identity; date/time and participation method; convening and notice/waiver evidence; attendance/proxies and quorum basis; exact decisions and vote totals where counted; elections/terms; disclosed conflicts, abstentions or recorded dissent; exhibits/financials presented; adjournment; approval and signature provenance. Distinguish “minutes drafted”, “approved” and “signed”; none by itself proves every underlying decision legally valid. **Confidence:** confirmed statutory record categories; detailed minute format is chiefly a practice/document rule.

## REC-2 — Retention, tax law and competing rules

| Record / entity | Confirmed rule | Practical interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| BC company accounting | BC-BCA **196(1)** and BC-BCR **20** prescribe **seven years**. | Do not replace with the commonly quoted CRA six-year period. |
| BC company governance records | BC-BCA **42–43**. Section43(1)'s seven-year provision permits specified older records to be held away from the records office subject to production within **48 hours** of request;43(2) permits compliant electronic accessibility. | Seven years is **not blanket destruction authority**. Backup is not the same as an accessible statutory register. ISC/transparency personal-data cessation/destruction rules are category-specific and must also be checked; don't preserve them indefinitely by borrowing a generic minute-book rule. **Confidence:** confirmed; document-by-document retention requires the longest applicable lawful period, with privacy minimization once no retention purpose remains.

## REC-5 — Court orders, consents and waivers

**BC company:** BC-BCA42 explicitly lists relevant court/registrar orders and director consent/resignation records among records-office requirements. Retain orders affecting articles, rights, meetings, directors, restoration or arrangement with their effective dates, affected records and evidence of required filing. Keep statutory waivers/consents with the proper resolution or minute-book category: e.g.169–170 notice,182 meeting matters,200 financial statements and203 auditor. Do not replace all-year, all-shareholder consent with the ordinary voting electorate.

**CBCA:**20's categories are not identical to BC42's enumeration. Court-approved reorganization/arrangement, articles, meetings and governance orders must be linked to the underlying proceeding and affected required records; avoid asserting that CBCA20 has BC's identical “all court orders” list. Director consent under106(9), written director/shareholder resolutions under117/142, notice waivers under136 and auditor waivers under163 should be retained as their corresponding statutory evidence.

**Ontario:**140 requires the operative constitutional/governance records;104/129 written resolutions, director consents under119(9)–(12), notice waiver under97 and auditor exemption under148 have distinct evidence. Orders affecting the corporation and its rights should be retained with the affected category and any required filings. Do not attach a BC-SA20 legal subtitle to an Ontario corporate court-orders register.

**Confidence:** confirmed statutory distinctions. A signature must identify who consented, in what capacity, to what text, when and for which financial year/meeting. Store source documents and review history. A later election or year does not retroactively prove an earlier consent.

**Written-consent chronology and Ontario notice implementation.** The revised workflow records the actual circulation timestamp, each actual consent timestamp and separate server audit timestamps. The frozen electorate and approval rule determine the chronological point at which the reviewed threshold was reached; entering evidence later does not move that legal event to the entry date. Additional evidence may move the established passage date earlier, with its prior conclusions preserved in an audit history. New circulation records are restricted to their actual UTC circulation date because the current member/director register cannot prove historical eligibility; legacy signed records remain preserved and require review.

OBCA **104(3)** starts its ten-business-day notice wording at the eligible-share majority, while **104(5)** explicitly modifies the passage requirements in **104(1)(c)(i)–(ii)** for higher governing-document thresholds. The app conservatively preserves the first statutory-majority event separately from a higher reviewed passage target and flags this interaction for review. This earlier reminder is an operational choice, not a claim that every possible articles/USA interaction is legally settled. Non-signers at that event have individual notice evidence with actual given-at timestamps. A later consent does not silently erase that recorded notice audience. Notice must include the resolution and the description and reasons in **104(4)**.

The app requires a reviewed due date and calendar basis before reporting notice timing as reviewed. A simple Monday–Friday count is inadequate: Ontario's [Legislation Act, 2006, ss 87–89](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06l21) includes its own holidays and applicable proclamations, while OBCA **1(8)** governs day-period calculation. The retrieved Legislation Act consolidation begins **2025-12-11** and runs to e-Laws currency; the exact end date was unavailable. Notice coverage, actual notice time, recording time, reviewed deadline and late delivery are separate fields. An earlier newly established majority date invalidates a deadline review tied to a different calendar day. Native Convex, MemoryDb and LocalStoreDb regressions exercise these rules, as well as duplicate/foreign evidence, immutable electorate weights and refusal to auto-carry under an unverified Act.

## REC-6 — Remuneration disclosure

**Ordinary private companies:** none of the ordinary BC BCA, CBCA or OBCA minute-book/annual-financial provisions establishes the BC society's general $75,000 employee/contractor remuneration-note rule. BC society BC-SA36 and BC-SR9 do not become applicable merely because a corporation operates in BC or is charitable. The ordinary corporate Employees page should not display that society-specific mandate.

**Important BC exception:** a **community contribution company** has a separate community-contribution-report requirement under BC-BCA **51.96**, record keeping under42(1)(q.1), and [Community Contribution Company Regulation, BC Reg 63/2013](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/63_2013) **9(1)(a)**. Its report discloses remuneration and position for persons meeting the $75,000 threshold, or the top ten where the statutory count condition applies. This is a different corporate-subtype rule, not grounds to re-enable the society note for every company. Regulation currency: current BC Laws consolidation to **2026-10-06**.

Public-company executive compensation disclosure, regulated industries, public-sector salary disclosure, charity T3010 reporting and contractual funding conditions are separate overlays. **Confidence:** confirmed baseline and CCC exception; subtype and overlays require explicit facts.

## Extensibility and evidence decisions

1. Keep entity identity, governing Act, public/private or special subtype, charity status, activity/workplace jurisdiction and governing-document version as separate facts.
2. Store notice electorate and voting electorate separately. Statutory auditor/director notice rights and non-voting-member notice rights must survive UI terminology changes.
3. Use distinct units: people, registered holders, voting shares, weighted votes, and cumulative vote budgets. Written resolutions can use a different unit from a meeting poll; see the corporate resolution analysis, especially Ontario104.
4. Keep dates with their legal basis and precision. Historical records need effective-dated ownership and legal cutoff/transfer-demand review. A creation timestamp is not a share-transfer effective date.
5. Treat dispatch, receipt, consent, waiver and statutory validity as separate states. Freeze the actual notice and voter evidence used for each event.
6. Retention and inspection are record-category decisions. Do not attach one entity's blanket visibility or deletion policy to every document.
7. The new tests verify weighted/cumulative ballots, holder/account separation, actor/tenant boundaries, duplicate prevention, frozen weights, notice recipients/coverage, non-share classification and calendar-month boundaries. They do not purport to prove unprovided articles or external legal facts.
