# BC societies: verified answers to SOC-1–SOC-16

Reviewed 11 October 2026. The BC Laws current consolidations of the [Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01) and [Societies Regulation, BC Reg 216/2015](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/216_2015) each display **current to 6 October 2026**. This is the publisher’s currency date, distinct from our review date. Statutory wording below is summarized unless expressly marked as a short quotation. Review historical transactions under the version then in force. “Settled core” means the stated general rule was verified, not that an organization’s facts or bylaws have been professionally approved.

<a id="soc-1"></a>

## SOC-1 — Director minimum and member-funded exception

**Rule:** An ordinary society needs at least three directors. A member-funded society needs at least one, and can have more under its bylaws.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [s. 40 and s. 197](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section197), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording:** Section 197(1) says “need have only one director”. Section 197(2) additionally disapplies s. 41, the restriction requiring a majority of directors not to be remunerated as employees or contractors. It does not remove qualifications, duties, conflicts, or consent requirements.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core; verify that member-funded status is legally available and appears in the constitution. A charity cannot simply toggle member-funded status to escape public-accountability requirements. The app now tests zero directors even for a member-funded society.

<a id="soc-2"></a>

## SOC-2 — BC residence

**Rule:** At least one director of an ordinary society must be ordinarily resident in BC. This requirement does not apply to member-funded societies.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 40, 197(1)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section40), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording:** The statutory phrase is “ordinarily resident in British Columbia”. Record the assessment; a postal address alone is not a conclusive residence determination.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. Do not reuse this boolean for the CBCA’s separately defined resident-Canadian test.

## SOC-3 — Consent, evidence and retention

**Rule:** Section 42(4) provides two routes: written consent under paragraph (a), or an appointment made at a meeting the individual attends without refusing under paragraph (b). A signed form is therefore not the exclusive valid route.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 42(4), 20(1)(f), 20(2)(a), 21–23](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section42), current to 2026-10-06. For electronic equivalents, the [Electronic Transactions Act, SBC 2001, c. 10](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/01010_01), ss. 5–9 and 11, supplies the electronic-record and signature framework; the record must remain accessible and the evidence must identify the consenting person and intention.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** A designation, election or appointment is invalid unless one of the two consent routes is met. Keep each written consent and resignation; keep meeting minutes documenting the alternative route. A missing upload is an evidence gap, not a finding that no consent occurred.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. Section 21 permits a required record to cease being retained only after ten years since creation or last alteration and only if no longer relevant. Do not purge ongoing appointment evidence automatically. An electronic implementation must preserve the actual consent, identity, date and record, not just a checked box.

## SOC-4 — Qualifications are not an annual renewal

**Rule:** Qualification requirements operate continuously under ss. 43–45. The Act does not require every director to sign an annual attestation.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 43–45](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section44); Societies Regulation, BC Reg 216/2015, [s. 10](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/216_2015#section10), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** Section 44(1) gives the ordinary minimum age of 18. Section 44(2) now addresses the regulatory exception for a 16- or 17-year-old, not the list of disqualifications. Regulation s. 10 requires enabling bylaws and a majority of adult directors. Disqualifications, including incapability, undischarged bankruptcy and specified convictions subject to statutory relief, are in s. 44(3).

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. Section 45 permits additional bylaw qualifications. Label annual attestations as optional governance practice unless the organization’s own binding rules require one; preserve the date and source of the applicable qualification version.

## SOC-5 — Required records and retention

**Rule:** Section 20(1) requires: incorporation certificate; certified constitution, bylaws, and director/office statements; other registry confirmations/certificates; court, tribunal and governmental orders; director register including names, contact details and appointment/cessation dates; written director consents/resignations; delivered director/senior-manager conflict disclosures; member register organized by class; general-meeting minutes with resolution text; other ordinary/special resolutions and written consents; required financial statements and any auditor’s report. Section 20(2) adds board minutes with attendees and resolution text, board consent resolutions and consents, and adequate accounting records.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 20–23](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section20), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** Section 20(1.1) limits the member register’s personal information to names and supplied contact information. Operational membership information must be separated from the inspectable statutory register. Section 21 uses a dual test: ten years since creation/last alteration **and** no ongoing relevance. Section 22 deals with location and electronic accessibility; s. 23 requires appropriate maintenance and reproducibility.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core; special student-society regulations and specific court orders require separate review. The same s. 21 floor applies across this list; concurrent tax, employment, privacy, litigation and historical-value requirements may require a longer period. A document-category match is only an evidence hint.

## SOC-6 — Inspection, copying and fees

**Rule:** Members inspect s. 20(1) records free, subject to the member-register restriction procedure in s. 25. Directors inspect all required records free. Bylaws can restrict members’ access to s. 20(2) board/accounting records but cannot remove access to the protected conflict-disclosure portions.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 24–25, 27–28, 195](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section24); Societies Regulation, BC Reg 216/2015, [ss. 4–7](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/216_2015#section4), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** Public inspection requires bylaw permission and never extends to the member register. A reasonable public-inspection fee is capped at $10 per day, regardless of the number of records. For an entitled requester, ordinary copying is capped at $0.50 per page or $0.10 per page by email. Directors receive required copies free; a member receives one free copy of current constitution/bylaws and latest financial statements. Copies are due promptly and no later than 14 days after request and applicable payment. Public requests for latest financial statements have a separate maximum of $10 plus the applicable per-page rate; member-funded public access differs under s. 195.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core; a board resolution restricting the member register must use s. 25 and does not extinguish permitted governance-purpose access. Reasonable notice/hours are allowed under s. 24(6); do not invent a single inspection response deadline from the separate 14-day copy rule.

## SOC-7 — Remuneration disclosure

**Rule:** For a society subject to s. 36, the financial statements include a remuneration note. All remunerated directors are covered; the $75,000 threshold concerns employees and contractors, not director remuneration.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 36, 196](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section36); Societies Regulation, BC Reg 216/2015, [ss. 8–9](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/216_2015#section8), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** Regulation s. 8 separates remuneration for being a director from remuneration in another capacity, with amount and role/capacity details. Regulation s. 9 permits either details for each employee/contractor paid at least $75,000 or their total number and aggregate remuneration. Names are optional.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. Section 196 exempts member-funded societies from s. 36. Preserve contractual and other disclosure obligations independently. Private corporations do not inherit this society remuneration note.

<a id="soc-8"></a>

## SOC-8 — Conflicts

**Rule:** Disclose the nature and extent of a material interest fully and promptly, abstain from the decision, leave during discussion and voting, and avoid influencing the outcome.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [s. 56(1)–(5)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section56), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** Evidence can appear in board minutes, a board consent resolution, or the specified record addressed to the directors and delivered/mailed to the registered office. Minutes are not the only permitted route.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core, with fact-specific review for materiality. Subsection (4) addresses the all-director-conflict case; (4.1) permits remaining to provide information when appropriately requested; (5) lists director remuneration/reimbursement, indemnity and insurance exceptions. The app must not assert that abstention and departure always apply without these exceptions.

## SOC-9 — AGM timing

**Rule:** Hold an AGM in each calendar year after the incorporation year. The current Act has no general 15-month interval rule for BC societies.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 71–72](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section71), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording:** Section 71(1) uses “held in each calendar year”. Incorporation-year exemption is s. 71(2). Apply by December 31 for a registrar-authorized postponement under s. 71(3); the specified date is ordinarily no later than March 31 of the following year, subject to a later prescribed date. A meeting held under that authorization belongs to the preceding calendar year, not both years.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. A unanimous written resolution can satisfy a deemed AGM under s. 72 only if it deals with all required annual business, including the financial statements and any auditor’s report. A scheduled date, a partial ordinary resolution, and an application for an extension are not proof of a completed meeting or granted extension.

## SOC-10 — Annual reports

**Rule:** Section 73(1) requires filing within 30 days after the actual or deemed AGM and inclusion of the meeting date. Section 73(2) requires a no-AGM report by January 31 of the following calendar year when an AGM required by s. 71(1) was not held.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [s. 73(1)–(4)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section73), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** The current provisions are **(1), (2), (3)**; the brief’s references to s. 73(1)(a)/(b) do not match this consolidation. Subsection (3) gives 30 days after the specified extended AGM deadline if that deadline was missed. No-AGM reporting must state that no AGM occurred. AGM director changes may be reported under s. 51(2).

**Variations/confidence:** Settled statute; portal fields are registry practice and should be checked at submission. A no-AGM report does not cure the meeting default. Two consecutive no-AGM annual reports permit the s. 73(4)/214 dissolution-warning process; do not predict immediate automatic dissolution or mark an overdue filing complete without evidence.

**Calendar qualification for SOC-9–SOC-10:** These are nominal Act dates. The current [BC Interpretation Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96238_01), ss. 2(1), 25(3)–(4) and 25.5, supplies holiday and office-closure rules unless a contrary intention applies. Its displayed currency is **2026-10-06**, reviewed **2026-10-11**; source ID `BC-IA-TIME`. For example, January 31, 2027 is Sunday: a February 1 filing must not be labelled legally late solely by comparing it with January 31. The implementation retains the nominal date and requires the applicable calendar evidence before an overdue/late legal conclusion. A filing remains factually recorded while its timeliness is under review. Notice boundaries before a meeting have a different direction and must not be moved forward automatically.

## SOC-11 — Constitutional and bylaw alterations

**Rule:** Ordinarily authorize the change by special resolution, then file the constitution or bylaw alteration application. The alteration takes legal effect on filing, not merely on the vote.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 1, 15, 17](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section15), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** Section 15(3) supplies the constitution effective date; s. 17(3) supplies the bylaw effective date. Sections 10–11 describe the documents, not the alteration procedure. At a meeting, the statutory special resolution baseline is at least two-thirds of votes cast. Written special resolutions require all voting members.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. Check valid higher thresholds, name reservation, registrar-ordered changes under s. 15(5), occupational-title provisions, and transition/previously unalterable provisions before applying a generic flow. Retain the special resolution, filing receipt, filed version and effective date separately.

## SOC-12 — Ordinary and special written resolutions

**Rule:** An ordinary written resolution must be circulated to every voting member and receive consent from at least two-thirds of the voting members. A special written resolution requires every voting member’s written consent.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [s. 1, definitions of ordinary resolution and special resolution](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section1), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** The denominator is all eligible voting members, not only those who return a form. An ordinary meeting vote instead uses a simple majority of votes cast. These are distinct procedures. Ten eligible voting members therefore require seven ordinary written consents, or ten special written consents.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. Section 54(2) governs directors’ written decisions and permits a valid bylaw departure from unanimity. Section 72 annual business requires unanimity even where its individual ordinary business could otherwise use two-thirds. Freeze the eligible register and prevent duplicate consents or arbitrary required-count reductions.

## SOC-13 — Notice window

**Rule:** The statutory general-meeting window is ordinarily 14–60 days. Bylaws can specify a minimum of at least seven days.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [ss. 77–78](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section77), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** This is in the Act, not merely the Model Bylaws. Notice goes to every member, including non-voting members. The special deemed-distribution route in s. 77(2) now uses more than 100 members and requires enabling bylaws plus the specified email and publication/posting combination.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. The notice must contain required business/resolution and participation information. Service and date-counting rules remain relevant; a platform timestamp alone is not proof of receipt or lawful service. Do not replace a calendar-month company rule with this day-based society window.

## SOC-14 — Model bylaws and quorum

**Rule:** Adopted Model Bylaw 3.7 sets general-meeting quorum at the greater of three voting members and 10% of voting members. Model Bylaw 5.5 sets directors’ quorum at a majority of directors.

**Citation/source:** Societies Regulation, BC Reg 216/2015, [s. 3 and Schedule 1, bylaws 3.6–3.9, 5.5](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/216_2015), current to 2026-10-06; Schedule 1 displays amendment by BC Reg 114/2022, Appendix 3. Societies Act, [s. 82](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section82) supplies the separate statutory general-meeting rule.

**Operative wording, paraphrased:** The Act’s default is three voting members, with a greater bylaw quorum, and all voting members if there are fewer than the applicable quorum. The 10% rule is not automatically imposed on every society.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled core. Check the filed/adopted version and any adjourned-meeting exception. Round a fractional headcount up; zero members never produce a valid meeting. Model Bylaw 3.15 prohibits proxies; enabling alternative bylaws can change that within the Act.

<a id="soc-15"></a>

## SOC-15 — Director-change filing

**Rule:** File changes to directors or their addresses promptly. A director change at the AGM may instead be included in the annual report.

**Citation/source:** Societies Act, SBC 2015, c. 18, [s. 51(1)–(2)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01#section51), current to 2026-10-06.

**Operative wording:** The timing word is “promptly”. The current Act does not impose the app’s stated generic 15-day or 30-day deadline for these society changes. Section 48 concerns cessation of office, not the filing rule.

**Variations/confidence:** Settled statute. Societies Online is the relevant registry filing workflow. The separate s. 73 annual-report deadline applies when using s. 51(2). Correcting an erroneous director statement is also prompt under s. 51(2.1).
