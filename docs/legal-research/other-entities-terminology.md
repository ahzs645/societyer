## OTH-1 — Federal nonprofit corporations under the CNCA

**Directors and membership.** CNCA s 125 requires at least one director; a soliciting corporation requires at least three, with at least two who are neither officers nor employees of the corporation or its affiliates. This is not the CBCA's resident-Canadian rule and is not the BC society resident-director test. Membership classes and voting rights belong in the articles; at least one class or group must vote, and the default is one vote per member unless the articles provide otherwise: CNCA s 154. A non-share corporation may have legal-person members. Neither member headcount nor voting power should be inferred from workspace accounts. [CNCA ss 125, 154](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-7.75/).

**Annual cycle.** CNCA s 160(1) and Regulations s 61 prescribe the first annual meeting within 18 months after incorporation and later annual meetings within 15 months after the previous meeting and six months after the preceding financial year end. Annual financial statements have their own requirements under ss 172–176. The corporate annual return is a separate duty under s 278: Corporations Canada's filing procedure requires it within 60 days following the incorporation, amalgamation or continuance anniversary, not 60 days after the annual meeting. [Regulations s 61](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2011-223/section-61.html); [official annual-return guidance](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/not-profit-corporations/information-annual-return-filings-not-profit-corporations).

**Soliciting status.** The test uses qualifying public funding received during the financial year, including specified outside donations, government funding and specified funds from other entities. The prescribed threshold is more than $10,000; excluded related donors and the exact statutory funding categories matter. Status begins at the following annual meeting and lasts until the third subsequent annual meeting under CNCA s 2(5.1) and Regulations s 16. It should be represented as a dated classification with supporting receipts, not as a synonym for registered charity. Soliciting status changes director composition, financial review and filing, residual-asset distribution and availability of a unanimous member agreement. [Regulations s 16](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2011-223/section-16.html); [Corporations Canada soliciting-corporation requirements](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/not-profit-corporations/requirements-soliciting-corporations-under-canada-not-profit-corporations-act-nfp-act).

| Revenue and classification | Financial review framework |
| --- | --- |
| Soliciting, $50,000 or less | Review engagement ordinarily applies; all members may waive appointment of a public accountant under the designated-corporation rules. |
| Soliciting, above $50,000 and up to $250,000 | Audit ordinarily applies; a special resolution may substitute a review engagement. |
| Soliciting, above $250,000 | Audit required under the statutory tier. |
| Non-soliciting, $1 million or less | Review engagement ordinarily applies; all members may waive appointment of a public accountant. |
| Non-soliciting, above $1 million | Audit required under the statutory tier. |

These are CNCA ss 179–190 and Regulations ss 80–84 tiers, subject to the precise financial-year test and any Director exemption. A waiver is evidence for its statutory period, not a permanent organization preference. Soliciting corporations also send annual financial statements to the Director under s 176. [Official financial statements and review guide](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/not-profit-corporations/financial-statements-and-review).

**What maps from a society:** member registers, member-class data, director/officer registers, annual governance records, minutes, consent evidence and the charity overlay are reusable concepts. BC notice periods, BC s 82 quorum defaults, the BC ordinary written two-thirds rule, BC remuneration disclosure and the BC annual-report formula do not map automatically. CNCA meeting notice and absentee voting must use its own ss 162 and 171 plus Regulations ss 63 and 74. The safe current runtime behavior is member terminology with an unverified-profile review state until these CNCA rules are separately implemented and tested. Confidence: high for this framework; conditional on soliciting classification, articles, bylaws and financial tier.

## OTH-2 — Ontario nonprofit corporations under ONCA

**Directors and member classes.** ONCA s 23 requires at least three directors. For a public benefit corporation, no more than one third may be employees of the corporation or an affiliate. Section 48 allows membership classes in the articles, requires at least one voting class and gives each member one vote unless the articles provide otherwise. These are member rights, including rights created for particular changes, not share-ledger rights. [ONCA ss 23, 48](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/10n15).

**Meetings, financial statements and return.** Section 52(1) requires a first annual meeting within 18 months after incorporation and later meetings within 15 months after the preceding annual meeting. Section 83(1)'s financial-statement period must end no more than six months before the meeting. Section 55(1) prescribes notice 10–50 days before the meeting to the members entitled to notice, directors and auditor or review-engagement provider. The Ontario annual return is a separate Corporations Information Act/registry obligation, normally due within six months after financial year end. It is not the CRA T3010 and does not follow BC's AGM-plus-30-days formula. [Official ONCA guide](https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-not-profit-corporations-act-2010); [Ontario not-for-profit and charitable-corporation rules](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rules-not-profit-and-charitable-corporations).

**Public benefit classification and financial tiers.** ONCA s 1 treats charitable corporations as public benefit corporations. A non-charitable corporation can enter that class through more than $10,000 in specified outside donations or government assistance in a financial year; the statutory timing rules in s 1(2) determine when the classification applies. The test and duration differ from CNCA soliciting status. Under s 76, a public benefit corporation with revenue of $100,000 or less may waive both audit and review by extraordinary resolution; above $100,000 and below $500,000 it may substitute a review for an audit; at $500,000 or more an audit applies. A non-public-benefit corporation with revenue of $500,000 or less may waive both; above that amount it may substitute review for audit. The s 76 waiver expires at the next annual meeting. The s 1 extraordinary-resolution definition uses at least 80% of votes cast at a meeting, or all voting members' written consent; it is not 80% of members merely present. [ONCA ss 1, 76](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/10n15); [official financial-review explanation](https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-not-profit-corporations-act-2010).

**Two distinct transitions.** ONCA came into force on 2021-10-19. Its ordinary three-year governing-document transition ended on **2024-10-18** under s 207. Inconsistent governing provisions are subject to the statutory deemed-amendment and exception rules; failure to rewrite every document by that date did not by itself dissolve an ordinary non-share corporation. Preserve letters patent, supplementary letters patent and the old bylaws as historical evidence, alongside the operative ONCA records. [Official transition considerations](https://www.ontario.ca/page/not-profit-corporations-act-2010-transition-considerations).

Share-capital social clubs remaining under the old Corporations Act have a **different five-year continuance requirement**, in Corporations Act s 2.1. That window ends **2026-10-18**, one week after this research date. Their permitted continuance routes include ONCA, OBCA and the Co-operative Corporations Act; these routes produce different governance and ownership models. Their status must not be silently converted by the generic nonprofit classifier or confused with the 2024 transition. [Corporations Act s 2.1](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c38).

**Implementation decision:** use member vocabulary and a dedicated ONCA candidate profile, with public-benefit classification, revenue period and transition evidence. Keep the BC-only automatic thresholds off. Confidence: high for the verified basic rules and official transition dates; medium for complete consolidation currency as disclosed in the source register.

## OTH-3 — Québec companies, the Enterprise Register and language

**Corporate governance.** Québec BCA s 106 requires at least one director; a reporting issuer requires at least three, at least two of whom are neither officers nor employees of the company or an affiliate. Section 108 incorporates individual-capacity and disqualification requirements rather than a Canadian-residency quota. Under ss 110–111, ordinary terms can be staggered within the applicable maximum, while cumulative voting requires an articles provision and has different election mechanics. Section 163 requires the first annual meeting within 18 months after incorporation and subsequent meetings within 15 months; s 225 requires the annual financial statements to cover a period ending no more than six months before the meeting. [Québec publisher text, ss 106, 108, 110–111, 163, 225](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/pdf/lc/S-31.1.pdf). These rules are verified in the 2025-09-01 consolidation; matching current text should be checked before enabling automated October 2026 deadlines.

**Annual updating declaration.** The annual declaration to the Registraire des entreprises is a separate enterprise-publicity duty. The official guide distinguishes a corporation whose annual updating declaration is linked to its income-tax return from a legal person whose declaration is not linked. An unlinked legal person's normal filing window is **May 15–November 15**. A linked corporation normally files within **six months after financial year end**, while the annual registration fee generally has its own **two-month** payment deadline. A no-change confirmation through the tax return and a separate online updating declaration for changes are different procedures. Record linkage status rather than assigning all Québec companies November 15. [Act respecting the legal publicity of enterprises, CQLR c P-44.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-44.1); [official annual updating guide, updated 2025-09-25](https://www.quebec.ca/entreprises-et-travailleurs-autonomes/mettre-a-jour-informations/declaration-mise-a-jour/annuelle).

**French-language requirements.** A Québec company name must satisfy the Charter of the French language: Québec BCA s 16 and Enterprise Publicity Act s 17 interact with the Charter's naming rules. Business, employment, contractual and public-facing communications have activity-specific French-language duties. Québec operations can make those duties relevant even where incorporation is federal or in another province. The Charter's s 19 rule requiring French meeting notices, agendas and minutes is specifically in the **civil-administration** regime; it is not a verified blanket rule requiring every private company's internal minute book to be exclusively French. No universal private-company French-only minutes rule was established by the sources inspected. Do not misapply s 19 or invent a private corporate-records requirement from s 91. [Charter of the French language, especially ss 16–19 and its employment/commerce chapters](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/C-11).

The product should support French originals, translated versions, document language and a reviewed communication-language basis. It must distinguish a private-company notice from an employment document, consumer contract, registry filing or public-administration record. Translation of menu labels does not by itself establish compliance with those separate duties. Preserve Québec's specific **règlement intérieur** terminology described in TRM-9. Confidence: medium for complete current-law coverage; high that civil-administration s 19 cannot simply be extended to every private corporation.

## OTH-5 — Legacy BC Acts and historical records

**BC company legacy alias.** “Business Corporations Act (British Columbia, legacy alias)” is a data alias for the current BC BCA when it identifies that same Act; it is not a second present-day corporate statute. Normalize to the BC BCA profile while retaining the original imported label and source. A record that actually predates the current Act should retain its historic instrument and event date. A current normalization must not rewrite the law that governed an old action. [BC Business Corporations Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02057_00).

**Pre-existing societies.** The 2015 Societies Act came into force on 2016-11-28, replacing the old Society Act; the general two-year transition period ended on 2018-11-28. Part 16, especially ss 231–245, governs legacy constitutions and records. The new constitution normally contains name and purposes; other legacy constitutional provisions were moved into bylaws on transition. Historical “unalterable” provisions must be preserved as evidence, but that old label does not establish a perpetual prohibition under current law: s 241.1 permits the relevant post-transition alteration mechanism. Government-related pre-existing societies can have additional Treasury Board approval requirements under s 245. Section 232.1 and other transitional provisions preserve specified historic rights and actions. [Current BC Societies Act, Part 16](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01).

Retain the pre-transition constitution/bylaws, transition application, resulting constitution/bylaws and their effective dates. Store whether a provision was historically unalterable and whether a special government-related approval remains applicable. A historical Society Act record is not grounds for putting today's organization on an obsolete, generic operational profile. Confidence: high for these distinctions; actual transition documents determine the organization-specific result.

## OTH-6 — Registered charity obligations across legal forms

**Annual information return.** Registered charity status adds the Income Tax Act s 149.1(14) information-return duty regardless of whether the entity is a society, nonprofit corporation, trust or other permitted form. The T3010 and required supporting information are due **six months after fiscal period end**. CRA's month-end examples matter: a February 28 year end maps to August 31, April 30 to October 31 and September 30 to March 31. A generic day-preserving six-month date shift would give the wrong results. Track this separately from the entity's corporate annual return. [ITA s 149.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-149.1.html); [CRA filing deadline examples](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/filing-t3010-charity-return/when-file.html).

**Books and records.** ITA s 230 and Income Tax Regulations s 5800 determine charity record retention, together with the corporate Act and other applicable law. CRA describes a general six-year retention rule, a two-year rule for copies of official donation receipts measured from the relevant calendar-year end, and continuing retention for governing documents and meeting minutes. Dissolution and revocation have distinct retention consequences; a six-year blanket delete policy is unsafe. Records must support reported amounts, charitable activities and eligible receipts, remain readable and be available to CRA. [ITA s 230](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-230.html); [Regulations s 5800](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._945/section-5800.html); [CRA charity records guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/books-records.html).

**Receipting.** Income Tax Regulations s 3501 specifies the information for official donation receipts, including the charity's identifying details and registration number, serial number, donor identity, relevant dates/place, gift and eligible amounts, advantage information, authorized signature and prescribed CRA reference. Non-cash gifts have additional description/value information. Preserve cancelled/replaced receipt history and evidence of authorization. An ordinary membership payment, service donation or payment with an advantage cannot be assumed to be fully receiptable; determine whether a qualifying gift and eligible amount exist. [Regulations s 3501](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._945/section-3501.html); [CRA official donation receipt requirements](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/issuing-receipts/what-information-must-on-official-donation-receipt-a-registered-charity.html).

T3010 financial-statement submission does not itself establish a universal CRA requirement for an external audit. The entity's incorporation statute, revenue category, funder agreements or another regulator may separately require audit or review. Charity status also has continuing charitable-purpose, spending and disbursement-quota implications under ITA s 149.1. This overlay should store registered status, registration number, fiscal period and relevant evidence; it should never replace the underlying legal entity profile. Confidence: high, conditional on actual registered charity status and the particular record/gift.

## TRM-1 — Annual General Meeting versus Annual Meeting

BC BCA **s 182** uses annual general meeting. CBCA **s 133** and OBCA **s 94** use annual meeting. Use those respective product labels, while allowing users to find familiar “AGM” shorthand in search and historical records; registry and practitioner shorthand does not change the governing procedure. BC society terminology also uses annual general meeting. Sources: [BC BCA s 182](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02057_06), [CBCA s 133](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-133.html), [OBCA s 94](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90b16). Confidence: high.

## TRM-2 — Articles, notice of articles and bylaws

For a BC company the ordinary statutory governing instruments are **articles and notice of articles**, with the incorporation agreement and other records where applicable: BC BCA ss 1, 12, 19 and 42. Calling the complete framework “articles only” loses the notice of articles. The Act does not provide the same general corporate-bylaw mechanism as CBCA s 103 or OBCA s 116. A BC company can still keep internal rules, policies, legacy documents or special-law instruments colloquially labelled bylaws; allow their preservation with an accurate document type, without presenting their amendment as the BCA's statutory governing-document process. Federal and Ontario business corporations have articles **and bylaws**. Sources: [BC BCA](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02057_00), [CBCA s 103](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-103.html), [OBCA s 116](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90b16). Confidence: high.

## TRM-3 — BC Articles alterations

BC BCA **s 257** uses alteration of articles; the related notice-of-alteration and effective-filing provisions include s 259. “Governance rules,” “Articles alterations” and “Articles history” are appropriate navigation labels when the underlying records distinguish articles from notice of articles and preserve actual filing names. The approving resolution, filed notice and resulting instrument should remain separate evidence. Avoid a BC statutory tab that assumes every governing change is a bylaw amendment. [BC BCA Part 9, ss 257–259](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02057_00). Confidence: high.

## TRM-4 — Federal and Ontario bylaw actions

CBCA **s 103** and OBCA **s 116** cover making, amending and repealing bylaws, with director adoption and shareholder confirmation/effect rules. “Bylaw amendments” is a useful navigation umbrella, but the stored action should identify **make**, **amend** or **repeal**, its effective date, submission to shareholders, and confirmation/rejection outcome. “Bylaws history” should show all three operations. [CBCA s 103](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-103.html); [OBCA s 116](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90b16). Confidence: high.

## TRM-5 — Special meetings and special business

“Special meeting of shareholders” closely matches CBCA **s 133(2)** and OBCA **s 94(1)(b)**. For BC companies, “general meeting other than an annual general meeting” is the more precise Act-based distinction; a company's articles may use special general meeting. A product may use “special meeting” as a plain-language label if the profile explains the Act's terminology. **Special business** is a separate classification under CBCA s 135(5) and OBCA s 96(5); annual meetings can contain special business. In BC, inspect the actual articles: the Regulation’s Table 1 articles 7.4 and 8.1 supply this classification when those articles apply, rather than BCA s 171 (which concerns record dates). A special meeting does not make every motion a special resolution. [BC BCA meeting provisions](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02057_06), [BC Regulation Table 1](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/65_2004), [CBCA s 135](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-135.html), [OBCA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90b16). Confidence: high for the federal/Ontario labels and the distinction; BC label remains articles-sensitive.

## TRM-6 — Ordinary, Special and unanimous consent

**Ordinary resolution and special resolution are defined in all three corporate Acts:** BC BCA s 1, CBCA s 2(1), OBCA s 1(1). “Ordinary” and “Special” are therefore sound kind labels. Keep the decision body, meeting/written procedure and actual threshold separate. A meeting majority is not a universal written-consent rule; BC's defined unanimous resolution and the federal/Ontario unanimous written-consent requirements remain meaningful and should be displayed when applicable. BC articles can set the special majority within the statutory range; special class approvals and annual waivers can demand another electorate. [BC BCA definitions](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02057_01), [CBCA s 2](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-2.html), [OBCA definitions](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90b16). Confidence: high. See the implemented per-Act written-resolution profiles for the exact approval rule.

## TRM-7 — Privacy labels

“Privacy” is the safe general navigation label. Show “BC PIPA” when the assessed activity falls under BC's Personal Information Protection Act; incorporation in BC by itself does not answer every privacy-jurisdiction question. For Québec, name the **Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector**, with a helpful reference to its Law 25 modernization. “Law 25” is the familiar amending-law label, not a replacement title for every operative provision. Federal PIPEDA, public-sector rules and other provincial laws can apply differently by activity and information. [BC PIPA](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/03063_01); [Québec private-sector privacy Act](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1); see the privacy research and activity-assessment profile in this revision. Confidence: high for the label principle; actual applicability is fact-dependent.

## TRM-8 — Voting holders, shares and votes

“Shareholders holding voting shares” is only a summary. More precise fields are **eligible shareholders at the voting record date**, **eligible voting shares**, and **votes represented**. Rights can differ by class, question, record date, transferee entitlement, proxy and articles; one person can hold several classes and an authorized representative can act for more than one holder. CBCA s 134/138, OBCA ss 95/100 and BC BCA ss 171–173, 177–179 govern different pieces of this framework. The app should show both legal-holder count and the correct weighted denominator, with the reviewed record date and rights basis. A user account or a member account is not a unit of corporate voting power. [CBCA s 138](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-138.html); [OBCA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90b16); [BC BCA meeting provisions](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/02057_06). Confidence: high; actual electorate is instrument- and event-dependent.

## TRM-9 — French labels

| Concept | Federal CBCA French | Québec BCA French | Product decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Governing articles | statuts | statuts | Preserve the specific filed instrument type where relevant. |
| Corporate bylaws/internal governance instrument | règlements administratifs | règlement intérieur | Select terminology by Act; do not globally translate every bylaws tab as règlements administratifs. |
| Articles amendment action | modification des statuts | modification des statuts | “Modifications des statuts” works as a history/page label. |
| Filed articles of amendment | clauses modificatrices | statuts de modification | Use the exact issuer's form name, not a generic page label copied onto the legal instrument. |
| Annual meeting; shareholders; directors; officers | assemblée annuelle; actionnaires; administrateurs; dirigeants | assemblée annuelle; actionnaires; administrateurs; dirigeants | Keep directors and officers distinct; preserve the governing Act's official French title. |

Federal **s 103** uses règlements administratifs; Québec **ss 6 and 113** use règlement intérieur. The official French federal Act is *Loi canadienne sur les sociétés par actions*; Québec's is *Loi sur les sociétés par actions*. “Société” in an English BC nonprofit category should not become a Québec share company merely through lexical matching. [French CBCA s 103](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/fra/lois/C-44/section-103.html); [French CBCA s 177](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/fra/lois/c-44/page-19.html?wbdisable=true), [Corporations Canada French forms glossary](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/fr/glossaire-loi-canadienne-pour-societes-actions), [Québec amendment-form guide](https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/emploi-solidarite-sociale/registraire_entreprises/REQ_GUI_Statuts_Modification_RE-500.G.pdf); [Québec BCA, ss 6, 113](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/S-31.1). Confidence: high for the statutory vocabulary, conditional for a particular form's title. Legal document templates still require a verified translation and their own applicable language analysis.

## Extending the runtime safely

Separate **legal form**, **governing Act**, **jurisdiction**, **charity status**, **public-funding classification**, **reporting/public status**, and **governing-document evidence**. A nonprofit corporation or cooperative should retain member authority unless its own Act and actual structure establish another basis. The runtime already prevents those explicit non-share Acts from accidentally selecting a generic corporate share-voting profile.

Each new automated profile should carry its own source IDs, actual consolidation date or an explicit unavailable state, retrieval/review date, conditional rules, evidence requirements and regression cases. For OTH-1/OTH-2, implement membership classes and the relevant public-funding/financial tier before enabling numeric audit or director checks. For Québec and the other provinces, close the stated currency/source gaps first. Generic document storage, member and director registers, records of meetings and evidence recording remain useful while a particular legal calculator is awaiting that verification; the UI must describe that scope accurately.
