## PRV-1 — Select laws by activities and information, not incorporation

**Rule.** Assess the operating territory, commercial activity, federal-work status and any sector-specific law. BC, Alberta and Québec have substantially similar general private-sector laws; PIPEDA can still govern federal works and interprovincial/international commercial handling, and normally covers commercial handling in provinces without such a general law.

**Citations.** PIPEDA, SC 2000, c. 5, ss. 2(1), 4(1), 26(2)(b); BC PIPA, SBC 2003, c. 63, ss. 1, 3; Alberta PIPA, SA 2003, c. P-6.5, ss. 4, 56; Québec private-sector Act, CQLR c. P-39.1, ss. 1, 3, and Civil Code art. 1525. The federal exemption orders are [BC, SOR/2004-220, s. 1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2004-220/FullText.html?txthl=exemption), [Alberta, SOR/2004-219, s. 1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2004-219/page-1.html), and [Québec, SOR/2003-374, s. 1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-374/page-1.html?txthl=exemption). Their retrieved operative text exempts covered intraprovincial handling, excludes federal works from that exemption and does not create a nationwide exemption for an entity incorporated in one province. Justice Laws shows each of the three orders current to 2026-09-21.

**Operative wording (paraphrase).** Commercial character is assessed for the transaction or activity; the organization is not classified exclusively by whether it distributes profit. Federal incorporation is not the federal-work test. See [OPC requirements in brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) and the [joint federal/provincial applicability guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/02_05_d_26/), read 2026-10-11.

| Assessed facts | General profile to consider |
| --- | --- |
| Private organization operating in BC, not a federal work | BC PIPA; commercial cross-border handling can add PIPEDA |
| Private organization operating in Alberta | Alberta PIPA, with the defined s. 56 nonprofit limitation; commercial cross-border handling can add PIPEDA |
| Enterprise operating in Québec | Québec private-sector Act; the enterprise test can include organized noncommercial economic activity |
| Commercial handling in Ontario, other provinces without general substantially similar legislation | PIPEDA, subject to applicable sector-specific exemptions |
| Federal work | PIPEDA for commercial information and covered employee/applicant information |
| Territorial private-sector activity | Assess the federal framework, including the OPC's treatment of territorial organizations as federally regulated. Noncommercial records stay conditional until federal-work/employee-information scope is assessed; territory alone does not certify ordinary member-data coverage. |
| Unknown operations or activities | Conditional profiles and missing-fact questions; no automatic statutory compliance result |

**Exceptions/variations.** Health information, public bodies, business contact information used solely for the qualifying purpose, personal/domestic activity and specified expressive purposes have separate rules/exclusions. PIPEDA does not generally cover a provincially regulated employer's employment information simply because its customers are governed by PIPEDA. Multiple laws and contractual requirements can coexist.

**Confidence.** Settled framework; particular federal-work, enterprise, territorial, health and cross-border facts may require legal review. The Alberta regulation currency limitation above applies.

**Implementation.** `resolvePrivacyProfile` reads explicit operating and activity fields, with no fallback from `jurisdictionCode`, `actFormedUnder` or the registered office address. Applicable and possible laws are separate arrays. Sector-specific or inconsistent facts remain conditional. The rule data and resources support multiple simultaneous profiles.

## PRV-2 — Noncommercial nonprofit member information

**Rule.** PIPEDA ordinarily does not cover a nonprofit's ordinary noncommercial membership administration. BC PIPA covers private societies' personal information even when the activity is noncommercial; provincial nonprofit treatment is not uniform.

**Citations and source.** PIPEDA ss. 2(1), 4(1)(a); BC PIPA ss. 1, 3. [OPC charitable and nonprofit guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/02_05_d_19/) and [BC OIPC private organizations](https://www.oipc.bc.ca/for-private-organizations/), read 2026-10-11, explain the application to membership records.

**Operative wording (paraphrase).** Collecting ordinary membership fees, organizing a club, maintaining the member list, ordinary newsletters and fundraising are generally noncommercial for this purpose. Selling, leasing or bartering donor or membership lists is expressly commercial. A nonprofit can also undertake other commercial activities.

**Exceptions/variations.** Alberta's s. 56(1)(b) class is (i) an organization incorporated under the Societies Act or the Agricultural Societies Act or registered under Part 9 of the Companies Act, or (ii) an organization that meets the criteria established under the regulations to qualify as a non-profit organization; operating on a nonprofit basis alone is insufficient. [OIPC Alberta's scope explanation](https://oipc.ab.ca/resource/minor-sports-associations/) distinguishes defined nonprofits from other nonprofit bodies. Québec applies an organized-economic-activity enterprise test, even where noncommercial: [CAI enterprise scope](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/champ-application-loi_entreprises).

**Confidence.** Settled distinctions; borderline commercial or enterprise activity is fact-dependent. **Implementation:** no blanket nonprofit exemption and no automatic PIPEDA membership checklist for an Ontario nonprofit. A fully assessed, noncommercial Ontario organization can receive “no general law identified for this handling,” with other duties preserved.

## PRV-3 — PIPEDA counterparts to the five BC checks

**Rule.** Where PIPEDA governs the records, accountability, privacy procedures, valid consent, access/correction and justified retention all apply. A linked policy file alone does not establish implementation.

| Check | Citation | Operative wording — paraphrase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Responsible individual | PIPEDA Schedule 1, cls. 4.1, 4.1.1 | Assign accountability to a designated individual or individuals. |
| Policy and complaints | Schedule 1, cls. 4.1.4, 4.8, 4.10 | Implement practices, safeguards, training and complaints procedures and disclose privacy practices. |
| Consent | s. 6.1; Schedule 1, cl. 4.3 | Meaningful consent requires understandable purposes and consequences, subject to statutory exceptions. |
| Access and correction | ss. 8(3)–(4), 9; Schedule 1, cl. 4.9 | Respond within 30 calendar days; apply lawful withholding, severance and extensions. |
| Retention | s. 8(8); Schedule 1, cls. 4.5–4.5.3 | Retain only for purposes and valid requirements; preserve a meaningful access opportunity and requested information through recourse. |

**Source/currency.** [Justice Laws PIPEDA consolidation](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html), current 2026-09-21. The clauses' exact mandatory/advisory language must be checked when extending enforcement, because s. 5(2) distinguishes provisions expressed as recommendations.

**Exceptions.** Section 7 supplies consent exceptions. Section 8(4) permits a bounded extension for interference/consultation and a necessary period for alternate-format conversion, with notice; it is not an unrestricted “extra 30 days.” Section 9 limits access. No universal fixed retention period or universal annual privacy attestation is created.

**Confidence.** Settled. **Implementation:** distinct `PIPEDA-*` citations, 30-calendar-day metadata, scoped policy content and access-log templates. Training review is identified as organizational practice rather than a fabricated annual legal certification.

## PRV-4 — Québec, including small enterprises and portability

**Rule.** The private-sector Act, as amended by Law 25, requires proportionate privacy governance, a responsible person, incident handling and assessments at the statutory triggers. Small size does not create a general exemption.

**Citation and operative wording (paraphrase).** CQLR c. P-39.1, s. 3.1 assigns the highest authority the role, permits written delegation and requires publication of contact details. Section 3.2 requires approved governance practices and published information; s. 8.2 adds a published policy for technological collection. Section 3.3 requires an assessment for the relevant system acquisition, development or overhaul, with privacy responsibility involved from the start; s. 17 separately addresses external-to-Québec handling and written agreements. Sections 3.5–3.8 address incidents; ss. 27 and 32 govern access/portability and response.

**Source/currency.** Official Act identified above (English consolidation updated to 2026-08-12). [CAI responsibilities](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/responsable-protection-renseignements-personnels-entreprise), [CAI PIA guide announcement](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/actualites/evaluation-facteurs-relatifs-vie-privee-guide-plus-convivial), and [CAI external handling guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/utilisation-communication-renseignements-personnels), read 2026-10-11, explain proportionality and procedures.

**Portability.** The amended s. 27 took effect **2024-09-22**, confirmed by [CAI's commencement notice](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/actualites/entree-en-vigueur-dispositions-portabilite-renseignements-personnels-informatises). It addresses computerized information collected from the applicant, not information created or inferred from it, in a common structured technological format. Serious practical difficulty is a statutory limit; a designated recipient must be legally authorized to collect it. The applicable access deadline is 30 calendar days, not the public-sector 20-day period.

**Exceptions/variations.** Proportionate governance and assessments are still mandatory at the relevant triggers. Access exceptions, identity requirements, legal retention and the criminal-investigation exception to individual incident notice remain relevant. The extra-Québec assessment can be triggered by a provider in another Canadian province. Pseudonymization is not automatically lawful anonymization under s. 23 and its regulation.

**Confidence.** Settled rules; enterprise status, adequate protection and serious practical difficulty require the actual facts. **Implementation:** Québec profiles and statutory citations; PIA, portability/access and incident-register draft records; no “small business exempt” switch. The label names the private-sector Act, with Law 25 described as its amending law.

## PRV-5 — Alberta differences that change the app

**Rule.** An Alberta profile needs its own scope, deadline, retention, foreign-provider and breach rules. Replacing “BC” with “Alberta” would give wrong results.

**Citations.** Alberta PIPA, SA 2003, c. P-6.5, ss. 5(3), 6, 7–10, 13.1, 24–28, 31, 34.1, 35, 37.1, 56; PIPA Regulation, Alta Reg 366/2003, ss. 19–19.1.

**Operative wording (paraphrase).** Designate responsibility and implement practices; respond to access/correction within **45 calendar days**, subject to extensions. Retention is tied to reasonable legal/business need. When using foreign service providers, policies and the prescribed notice must address the countries/purposes and how the individual can obtain information and ask questions.

**Official evidence.** [OIPC access procedure](https://oipc.ab.ca/how-to-make-an-access-request/) expressly identifies the 45-calendar-day period. [OIPC's 2024 order listing](https://oipc.ab.ca/decisions/orders/) describes s. 5(3), s. 6 and s. 35 compliance requirements. [OIPC foreign-provider investigation](https://oipc.ab.ca/p2010-ir-02/) explains ss. 6(2) and 13.1. [Breach guidance](https://oipc.ab.ca/breach-notification/) explains the statutory reporting and individual-notification sequence. All were read 2026-10-11; the publisher-consolidation limitation is stated above.

**Exceptions/variations.** Apply the s. 56 nonprofit classification, statutory consent exceptions and sector-specific rules. BC's one-year decision-retention floor is not copied into Alberta's general retention rule. The Commissioner may require individual notification; organizations may notify voluntarily without waiting.

**Confidence.** The King's Printer consolidation of the Act (current as of 2025-09-01) and official regulator guidance support these rules; the regulation's currency remains to be verified. **Implementation:** separate `AB-PIPA-*` citations, 45-calendar-day access metadata, conditional nonprofit scope and no fabricated breach-log retention minimum.

## PRV-6 — Breach notices and incident records

**Rule.** The four regimes have different reporting duties and retention triggers. Never implement a uniform 72-hour deadline or a universal two-year incident-retention period.

| Regime | Notification threshold and timing | Register / retention | Citation and official guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PIPEDA | Assess real risk of significant harm. Notify the OPC and affected individuals as soon as feasible after determination; related harm-reducing notices may also be required. | Record **every** covered breach, including those not reported. Keep at least **24 months after determining the breach occurred**. | PIPEDA ss. 10.1–10.3; SOR/2018-64, ss. 2–6; [OPC guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/privacy-breaches-at-your-business/gd_pb_201810/) |
| Alberta PIPA | Report to Commissioner without unreasonable delay where the objective real-risk-of-significant-harm threshold is met. Follow individual-notification directions; voluntary notice is permitted. | Record the risk decision and response as prudent evidence. No fixed general all-breach-log period identified in the reviewed regime. | PIPA ss. 34.1, 37.1; Regulation ss. 19–19.1; [OIPC guidance](https://oipc.ab.ca/breach-notification/) |
| BC PIPA | No general mandatory private-sector breach-reporting provision in the consolidation reviewed. Reporting to OIPC and people at risk is recommended; other legal/contractual duties can apply. | Evidence and incident-response logs recommended; no fixed general PIPA incident-log period. | PIPA s. 34; [BC OIPC reporting guidance](https://www.oipc.bc.ca/resources/breach-notification-representatives-of-organizations-and-public-bodies/) explicitly distinguishes private organizations from mandatory public-body reporting |
| Québec private-sector Act | Prompt CAI and individual notice where the incident risks serious injury; assess sensitivity, consequences and likelihood. Mitigate harm and recurrence. | Register **all** confidentiality incidents. Keep information current for **at least five years after awareness**. | Act ss. 3.5–3.8; incident regulation ss. 7–8; [CAI incident guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/incidents-confidentialite-mesures-securite-entreprises) |

**Operative exact wording, limited extract.** PIPEDA s. 10.1 uses “real risk of significant harm”; Québec uses a risk of serious injury. These are distinct statutory tests, not interchangeable field labels.

**Exceptions.** Check statutory alternatives to direct notice, investigation-related restrictions, source-of-control questions and concurrent regimes. The breach date, awareness date and determination date may differ. A longer legal hold can supersede a minimum retention end date.

**Confidence.** Settled comparisons, with the Alberta regulation currency limitation. **Implementation:** per-law breach prompts and minimum-month metadata (federal 24, Québec 60, BC/Alberta unspecified), separate awareness/determination fields in the incident-register template, no automatic deletion or notification.

## PRV-7 — C-27, CPPA and successor legislation

**Rule.** Do not cite the CPPA proposed in C-27 as operative law. Keep proposed reform separate from PIPEDA requirements.

**Official status read 2026-10-11.** [C-27 (44-1), LEGISinfo](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/44-1/c-27) records a session ending **2025-01-06** and an incomplete committee stage; it never completed passage. [C-36 (45-1), LEGISinfo](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-36) identifies the successor proposal, **Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act**, first read **2026-06-15**, with status **at second reading in the House of Commons**. The [bill text](https://www.parl.ca/DocumentViewer/en/45-1/bill/C-36/first-reading) proposes replacing Part 1 of PIPEDA; a proposal is not commencement.

**Exact wording / exceptions.** Bill names and recorded stages above are metadata, not an in-force statutory obligation. Royal assent and relevant commencement provisions must be checked before implementing any reform, including separate PIPEDA amendments in other bills.

**Confidence.** Settled for the retrieved status, time-sensitive thereafter. **Implementation:** `PRIVACY_REFORM_WATCH` is separate from `PRIVACY_LAWS`, and the proposed bill has `inForce: false`. It is never used to fail a compliance check or populate operative policy duties.

## PRV-8 — CASL newsletters, shareholder messages and meeting notices

**Rule.** Determine whether the message encourages commercial activity, then whether a particular consent basis or exemption applies. Neither shareholder status nor the word “nonprofit” is a blanket permission to send marketing.

**Citations.** CASL, SC 2010, c. 23, ss. 1(2), 6(1)–(2), 6(6), 10(9)–(14), 11, 13; Electronic Commerce Protection Regulations, SOR/2013-221, ss. 3(c), 3(g), 7. Consolidations above are current to 2026-09-21.

| Message / relationship | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Noncommercial newsletter | First assess whether it is a commercial electronic message at all. Informational messages are not automatically marketing. |
| Commercial membership newsletter | A qualifying defined club/association/voluntary organization may have implied consent through its existing nonbusiness relationship. Verify membership requirements and the relevant period, including the statutory treatment of termination. Identification and unsubscribe remain required unless another full exemption applies. |
| Shareholder newsletter with promotion | Shareholding alone does not prove a consent basis. A qualifying investment opportunity, contract, express consent or another statutory basis must be assessed and recorded. |
| Notice satisfying a legal obligation | Regulation s. 3(c) can exclude the message from s. 6; statutory meeting-notice delivery and audience rules still apply. Adding marketing warrants a fresh classification. |
| Transactional message under s. 6(6) | This is a consent exception for the listed solely transactional purposes; do not treat it as removing every identification/unsubscribe requirement. |
| Registered-charity fundraising | Regulation s. 3(g) applies where the primary purpose is fundraising for the registered charity. It is distinct from a general nonprofit or membership rule. |

**Operative wording (paraphrase).** Express consent continues until withdrawn; implied consent may expire. Keep evidence of its basis and scope. The statutory unsubscribe maximum is ten business days, and the mechanism must remain valid for at least 60 days after the message.

**Official guides.** [CRTC FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm), [CRTC implied-consent guide](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm?wbdisable=true), and [CRTC recordkeeping/time-limit infographic](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/infograph.htm), read 2026-10-11. These clarify the defined membership class and the distinction between consent and remaining message requirements.

**Confidence.** Settled framework; message content and consent evidence are factual judgments. **Implementation:** privacy drafts separate marketing from required notices and require basis/evidence/expiry rather than treating a stored preference as proof. This change does not silently expand mailing audiences or send messages. Existing transport controls remain separate from this legal classification.

## TRM-7 — Privacy module label

Name BC PIPA only when its application to the assessed records is supported. Name Alberta PIPA distinctly. For Québec use “Québec private-sector privacy law” (or the Act's full title), explaining that Law 25 amended it; for PIPEDA use PIPEDA. With unresolved facts, show “Privacy — applicability needs assessment.” Mixed profiles display multiple law labels rather than picking one from incorporation.

**BC PIPA presumption for BC-home workspaces.** BC PIPA s. 1 defines “organization” to include a not-for-profit organization (alongside a person, an unincorporated association, a trade union and a trust), and s. 3(1) applies the Act to every organization, subject to s. 3(2) and to the federal Act where it governs the handling (for example, a federal work, undertaking or business, or interprovincial or international commercial transfers, under the BC exemption order SOR/2004-220). A BC society or BC company handling personal information in BC is therefore within BC PIPA unless facts displace it. The app does not leave such a workspace unassessed: for a workspace whose home jurisdiction is BC (`organizationRuleScope(...).isBcHome`), `resolvePrivacyProfile` treats BC PIPA as applicable with basis `presumed`, status `presumed` (or `conditional` once some facts are recorded), and the label “Privacy (BC PIPA — presumed: confirm facts)”. The BC PIPA dashboard checks run, and the “Assess privacy applicability” warning says BC PIPA is presumed and asks for the displacing facts rather than stating that incorporation jurisdiction establishes nothing. Citation: `PIPA-SCOPE` (ss. 1, 3(1)–(2)).

The presumption is displaced, and the recorded facts decide, when the assessment records any of: a federal work, undertaking or business (`privacyFederalWork: "yes"`, which routes to PIPEDA); a health or public-sector regime (`privacySectorSpecific: "health" | "public"`); or operating locations that do not include BC. Recording BC operations with federal-work status “no” and no sector-specific regime converts the presumption into a recorded “applies” assessment. Interprovincial or international commercial handling still adds PIPEDA alongside BC PIPA. The presumption is limited to BC: incorporation jurisdiction never presumes Alberta PIPA, the Québec Act or PIPEDA, and a non-BC workspace with no recorded facts stays unassessed.

## Implementation and extension contract

- `shared/privacyRuleProfiles.ts`: explicit scope facts, conditional resolution, source metadata, requirements, response-day bases, incident-retention minimums and separate reform watch.
- `shared/privacyPolicyTemplate.ts`: a single policy generator used by both client rebuild and hosted/offline creation. It uses saved scope, leaves unresolved facts visible and preserves authored drafts until a deliberate rebuild.
- `shared/privacyRecordTemplates.ts`: incident register, request log and privacy-impact-assessment drafts with statutory triggers and evidence fields.
- `src/components/PrivacyScopeAssessment.tsx` and `src/pages/Privacy.tsx`: editable scope, multiple provincial profiles, conditional sources, scoped policy and operational-record drafting.
- `convex/lib/privacyValidators.ts`, `convex/schema.ts`, `convex/society.ts`: optional additive fields; no stored data is migrated. Existing non-BC workspaces remain unassessed; BC-home workspaces resolve to the labelled BC PIPA presumption above until facts are recorded.
- Privacy setup motions retain stable seeded keys for idempotence; their statutory wording depends on the assessed profile.

A new profile must provide a source, actual consolidation date or an explicit limitation, applicability tests, relevant exceptions, statutory-versus-recommended classification, response-day basis and retention trigger. Add regression cases for applicable, excluded, incomplete and overlapping facts. Do not encode proposed legislation in the active-law registry or copy a provincial deadline into another profile.
